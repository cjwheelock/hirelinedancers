# Hire Line Dancers SEO, AI discovery, and authority strategy

Last updated: August 2, 2026

## Current decision

Hire Line Dancers will not bulk publish localized blog posts or run an automatic weekly city-article calendar.

The earlier 50-week calendar is retired. Existing city drafts are research material only. They are disconnected from the production blog and must not publish automatically.

The goal is not to create the largest number of pages. The goal is to become the most credible and useful place to find a line dance instructor for an event.

## Launch markets

The marketplace begins with 11 markets:

1. Nashville, Tennessee
2. Fort Worth, Texas
3. Austin, Texas
4. Dallas, Texas
5. Houston, Texas
6. San Francisco Bay Area, California
7. New York City metro, New York
8. Phoenix and Scottsdale, Arizona
9. Atlanta, Georgia
10. Charlotte, North Carolina
11. Las Vegas, Nevada

Denver is not a launch market. Existing Denver research and outreach records should be replaced with San Francisco Bay Area and New York City metro records.

## Strategic principle

There is no separate shortcut for generative engine optimization. The same qualities that help a page earn search visibility also help AI products understand and cite it:

- Clear authorship and a real About page
- Accurate first-party marketplace data
- Real instructor profiles
- Original photos, videos, and interviews
- Useful answers to specific organizer questions
- Visible review and update dates
- Descriptive internal links
- Crawlable public pages
- Structured data that matches visible content
- Independent mentions and links earned through useful work

AI can support research, editing, quality checks, and structured data. AI should not manufacture local expertise, reviews, quotes, or repetitive city pages.

## What should be indexed

### Market pages

Each launch-market page should remain public and indexable. It should contain:

- A distinct market introduction
- Geographic service-area information
- Local planning considerations
- Published instructor profiles serving that area
- Relevant event types
- Practical contact guidance
- Links to any genuinely useful local resources

A market page should not claim that instructors are available until at least one real instructor can serve that area. Empty markets may remain as transparent launch pages, but the copy must say that applications are being reviewed.

### Instructor profiles

Real instructor profiles are the strongest source of unique marketplace content. Each profile can include:

- Name and business name
- Main profile photo
- One to three additional images
- One welcome video
- One to three teaching or dancing videos
- Bio and teaching experience
- Home market and travel radius
- Styles taught
- Event types accepted
- Group-size comfort
- Equipment provided
- Insurance availability
- Favorite line dance song and Spotify link
- Response-time commitment
- Accurate profile update date

Do not publish private rates, phone numbers, personal email addresses, fabricated ratings, or unverified credentials.

### Event pages

Event pages should answer the questions organizers have before contacting an instructor. They should explain fit, logistics, timing, sound, space, participation, and the information needed for a useful quote.

### Guides and case studies

National guides and real booking case studies can be more valuable than dozens of city posts. Priority resources include:

- What it costs to hire a line dance instructor
- What information to include in an inquiry
- Sound, microphone, and floor-space checklist
- Corporate event run-of-show examples
- Wedding and bar or bat mitzvah planning checklists
- How instructors handle mixed ages and first-time dancers
- How to start a college line dancing group
- Real event case studies with organizer and instructor permission

## Publication gate

An article may be published only when every answer below is yes:

1. Does it answer a real question from an organizer, instructor, or dancer?
2. Is it meaningfully different from existing pages?
3. Does it contain first-party knowledge, original data, an interview, or verified primary sources?
4. Can every local or factual claim be supported?
5. Does it have a named author and review date?
6. Does it help someone make a decision or complete a task?
7. Does the call to action match what the marketplace can currently deliver?
8. Would the page still be worth publishing if search engines did not exist?

If any answer is no, keep the material as a draft.

## Publishing cadence

There is no fixed weekly quota.

During the marketplace launch, a reasonable target is zero to two high-quality articles per month. Publishing should happen only when new evidence, interviews, original data, or a completed case study makes the page useful.

The most important content work for the next 90 days is:

1. Publish real instructor profiles in the 11 markets.
2. Improve event and market pages using questions from actual inquiries.
3. Interview several instructors about quoting, equipment, insurance, group size, and response expectations.
4. Turn repeated organizer questions into one authoritative national guide.
5. Document the first successful bookings as permissioned case studies.
6. Add original photos and videos supplied by instructors.
7. Measure which pages generate qualified inquiry starts and completed inquiries.

## Topic selection score

Before drafting, score a topic from zero to two on each dimension:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Audience need | No observed need | Plausible question | Repeated real question or query data |
| Original evidence | None | Primary sources | Interview, first-party data, or case study |
| Marketplace relevance | Weak | Related | Directly supports a profile or inquiry |
| Distinctiveness | Repetitive | Some unique value | Clearly unavailable elsewhere |
| Maintenance burden | Quickly stale | Periodic review | Durable or easy to maintain |

Draft only topics scoring at least seven out of ten. A score of zero for original evidence blocks publication regardless of the total.

## Technical SEO and AI discovery

Maintain:

- Public browsing without a login wall
- Authentication only when a user contacts an instructor or manages an account
- One canonical URL per page
- Accurate XML sitemap modification dates
- RSS for genuinely new editorial content
- Descriptive titles and summaries
- Organization, ProfilePage, Service, Article, and breadcrumb structured data only when visible content supports it
- Search-friendly HTML for market, event, guide, and profile pages
- Fast mobile layouts and accessible forms
- `robots.txt` rules that permit legitimate search and AI discovery crawlers
- `llms.txt` as a supplemental summary, not as a substitute for useful pages
- IndexNow notifications only for published, materially updated, or removed URLs

Do not create fake local business listings, virtual offices, doorway pages, hidden keyword text, or city-name variants with substantially identical copy.

## First-party data opportunities

Once the marketplace has enough activity, publish aggregated findings such as:

- Most requested event types
- Typical travel radii
- Common group-size ranges
- Equipment instructors most often provide
- Organizer questions that predict a qualified inquiry
- Instructor response-time benchmarks
- Percentage of inquiries that receive a response
- Self-reported booking outcomes

Document the methodology and protect personal information. Do not publish statistics from a sample too small to be meaningful.

## Natural authority and links

Backlink outreach and automated outreach are deferred until the marketplace has real profiles and useful first-party resources.

When this work resumes, prioritize natural reasons to link:

- Instructor profile launch kits
- Contributor interviews
- Permissioned event case studies
- College club resources
- Original marketplace research
- Useful checklists for planners, venues, DJs, and instructors
- Local corrections and contributions

Do not buy links, exchange links at scale, use low-quality directories, or send bulk generic link requests.

## Safe agent automation

Agents may automate:

- Search Console and analytics summaries
- Research packets from primary sources
- Interview-question preparation
- Transcription and quote organization
- Broken-link and stale-fact checks
- Internal-link suggestions
- Metadata and structured-data validation
- Duplicate-language detection
- Draft outlines based on verified evidence

Agents must not automatically:

- Publish city articles
- Invent quotes, reviews, local observations, or prices
- Send outreach campaigns
- Create external accounts or listings
- Submit guest posts or comments
- Buy or trade links
- Represent an instructor, organizer, or venue without permission

## Measurement

Primary measures:

- Published instructor profiles by market
- Profile completeness
- Profile views
- Inquiry starts and completed inquiries
- Instructor email and SMS delivery
- Instructor acknowledgment and response status
- Organizer-reported response receipt
- Self-reported and confirmed booking outcomes
- Qualified inquiries per market
- Membership activation and retention

Supporting search measures:

- Indexed pages
- Search impressions and clicks
- Search queries and landing pages
- Branded versus non-branded discovery
- Relevant referring domains
- AI assistant and search referral visits where identifiable
- Page review age and stale-content flags

Traffic alone is not success. The business goal is more qualified event inquiries, more instructors hired, and more people dancing.
