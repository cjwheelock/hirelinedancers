<p>The best events give guests something to do, not just something to see. Here are five interactive entertainment options that reliably work — with honest notes on where each fits best.</p>
<h2>1. Line dance instructor</h2>
<p>One pro turns your whole guest list into the entertainment. Best for: weddings, corporate parties, reunions — any mixed crowd. Works for 10 to 500+ people, needs no equipment beyond sound, and every guest can join regardless of age or ability. The finale doubles as your best photo op. <a href="/#find">Find one near you</a>.</p>
<h2>2. Casino night tables</h2>
<p>Blackjack and craps tables with funny money get strangers talking. Best for: corporate mixers and fundraisers. Watch for: cost scales with table count, and it splinters the room into small groups rather than uniting it.</p>
<h2>3. Photo booth or 360 camera</h2>
<p>Always popular, low effort for guests. Best for: any event as a secondary activity. Watch for: it entertains two to four people at a time — it's a station, not a centerpiece.</p>
<h2>4. Trivia or game show host</h2>
<p>Structured fun with team competition. Best for: corporate groups that know each other. Watch for: seated and screen-based; energy peaks early.</p>
<h2>5. Karaoke with a live host</h2>
<p>High-energy and hilarious with the right crowd. Best for: smaller parties of extroverts. Watch for: participation is voluntary and uneven — a few stars, many spectators.</p>
<h2>The takeaway</h2>
<p>Stations entertain clusters; shared activities unite rooms. If your goal is one moment everyone experiences together, a guided group dance is the most reliable way to get it.</p>
