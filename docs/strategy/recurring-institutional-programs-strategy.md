# Hire Line Dancers Recurring Institutional Programs Strategy

**Status:** Working strategy and pilot plan
**Version:** 1.0
**Date:** August 4, 2026
**Owner:** Hire Line Dancers
**Planning horizon:** 12 months

## Executive decision

Hire Line Dancers should develop a managed-program agency alongside its existing instructor directory.

The managed-program business would sell recurring line-dance programming to institutions, contract with participating instructors at preset program rates, and remain accountable for scheduling, backup coverage, client communication, billing, quality, and reporting.

The core promise is:

> One partner, one invoice, a qualified instructor, and a beginner-friendly program people want to attend again.

The initial goal is not national scale. The initial goal is to prove that one metro can support a dense, reliable portfolio of recurring clients with at least 25 percent contribution margin and strong renewal.

## Strategic architecture

The company should keep two commercial models visibly separate.

| Business line | Customer relationship | Instructor relationship | HLD revenue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Instructor directory | Organizer contacts and contracts with the instructor directly | Instructor sets rates and terms | Current $14.99 instructor membership, no event commission |
| Managed Programs | Institution contracts with Hire Line Dancers for a defined program | Instructor opts into preset program scope, pay, and service standards | Setup fee plus recurring agency program revenue |

The directory is a lead-generation marketplace. Managed Programs is a service business. The agency margin pays for real work: institutional sales, matching, contracting, invoicing, client success, program structure, reporting, and replacement coverage.

This creates the larger mission flywheel:

1. An ordinary dancer finds line dancing through the free Hey Sammy partner experience.
2. An enthusiast starts a club or develops into an instructor.
3. Hire Line Dancers assesses and supports program-ready instructors.
4. Institutions buy reliable recurring programs.
5. Instructors receive repeat paid work.
6. Strong instructors become mentors and help new communities launch.

## Operating principles

1. **Sell an outcome, not an instructor hour.** Buyers should purchase participation, consistency, and reliable delivery.
2. **Standardize the core.** Use one program framework with a small number of segment-specific configurations.
3. **Protect instructor economics.** Use fixed session pay during the pilot and preserve pay when clients cancel late.
4. **Do not launch a market without backup.** Every active program needs a primary instructor and an oriented substitute.
5. **Avoid custom-work creep.** Pricing, reports, playlists, and policies need defined boundaries.
6. **Use paid pilots.** A free pilot does not validate willingness to pay or sustainable delivery.
7. **Start with adults.** Youth and camp programs add meaningful screening and safeguarding requirements and should follow the adult-program pilot.
8. **Do not confuse activity with impact.** Track attendance, repeat participation, satisfaction, renewal, and financial contribution.

## Standardized offers

All prices are pilot assumptions, not public commitments. They should be validated through instructor interviews, buyer conversations, and paid proposals. Local travel, high-cost markets, group size, equipment, and procurement can change the price.

### Offer 1: Community Weekly

**Best for:** senior living, apartment communities, community centers, fitness centers, and parks programs.

- Four beginner-friendly 45 to 60-minute sessions per month
- Up to 40 participants
- Primary instructor plus oriented backup
- Shared program framework and adaptation notes
- Client launch copy and simple promotional assets
- Monthly attendance and feedback recap
- Three-month initial term

**Pilot price:** $1,500 per month plus $350 setup
**Instructor pay assumption:** $225 per session plus $25 local travel
**Estimated contribution margin:** 26 percent

### Offer 2: Workplace Wellness

**Best for:** employers, coworking communities, associations, and employee resource groups.

- Four 45-minute sessions per month
- Up to 60 employees
- Beginner-friendly format with no partner or special attire required
- Internal promotional kit
- Quarterly participation and satisfaction report
- Optional kickoff or company celebration format
- Three-month initial term

**Pilot price:** $1,800 per month plus $500 setup
**Instructor pay assumption:** $250 per session plus $25 local travel
**Estimated contribution margin:** 32 percent

### Offer 3: Campus Club Builder

**Best for:** campus recreation, student activities, residence life, and university wellness.

- One 90-minute kickoff event
- Eight weekly beginner classes
- One student-leader practice lab
- Campus club starter resources
- Up to 75 students in standard delivery
- Primary instructor, backup, and kickoff assistant
- End-of-semester participation recap and renewal plan

**Pilot price:** $5,000 per semester, including $500 setup
**Instructor pay assumption:** $350 kickoff, $225 per weekly class, $225 leader lab, travel, and one kickoff assistant
**Estimated contribution margin:** 37 percent

### Offer 4: Venue Residency

**Best for:** bars, breweries, entertainment venues, hotels, and resorts.

- Four 90-minute public lessons per month
- Up to 100 participants before an assistant is required

- Venue promotional kit
- Primary instructor plus oriented backup

**Pilot price:** $2,200 per month plus $500 setup
**Instructor pay assumption:** $325 per session plus $25 local travel
**Estimated contribution margin:** 30 percent

### Add-ons

Keep the add-on menu short.

| Add-on | Client price | Instructor allocation or rule |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Additional assistant | $225 per session | $150 paid to assistant |
| Custom choreography or branded routine | From $350 | 70 percent allocated to instructor or choreographer |
| Expanded travel | Quoted | Primarily passed through |
| Launch in fewer than 10 business days | 15 percent premium | Used to fund rush coordination |

### Segment-specific pitch

**Senior living**
"A safe, adaptable weekly social-movement program with reliable instructors, substitutions, and options for different abilities." Avoid medical claims. Align the program with each operator's resident interests, abilities, and care policies. Federal long-term-care guidance emphasizes activities that reflect resident interests and support physical, mental, and psychosocial well-being [S7].

**Bars and venues**
"Build a recurring midweek community, then measure lesson attendance, conversion into open dancing, and venue spend." The venue is buying consistency and a reliable crowd-building format, not only an instructor.

**Community and fitness centers**
"Add a social format that can attract participants who may not identify as traditional gym users while supporting participation and retention." Expect certification, CPR/AED, background, audition, substitute, and incident requirements to vary by facility.

**Apartments**
"Activate an underused amenity with a program that gets neighbors interacting and gives the property useful resident content." A portfolio relationship can expand one successful property into several locations.

**Parks and recreation**
"Offer a catalog-ready class with a prepared description, enrollment threshold, beginner progression, and reliable substitutes." Municipal contract-class programs commonly use revenue sharing. Published examples range from a 65 percent contractor share at city facilities in Burbank to a usually negotiated 80 percent contractor share in South Whidbey [S1, S2]. These are examples, not universal standards.

**Resorts and hotels**
"Create a repeatable signature guest activity that works for mixed ages and walk-ins, supported by a local instructor network that protects the published schedule." Backup reliability and brand standards matter as much as choreography.

**Corporate**
"Give employees a high-participation activity requiring no partner, equipment, athletic background, or special attire." Corporate wellness and workplace-event guidance supports group activity and social-support approaches, but the program should report participation and satisfaction rather than promise health outcomes [S8].

**Universities**
"Launch a no-partner social activity that creates visible belonging and leaves the campus with student leaders who can help sustain it." Expect performance agreements, vendor onboarding, insurance, and long review timelines [S3, S4, S6].

**Camps**
"Activate the whole camp without specialized equipment, then teach counselors how to repeat the activity." Youth delivery should not launch until background screening, safeguarding, supervision, incident, and consent processes are reviewed. American Camp Association standards include contracted personnel in screening guidance [S9].

## Instructor network and quality standard

A published directory profile does not automatically make an instructor eligible for managed programs. Managed Programs should have a separate opt-in pool and readiness review.

Minimum program-ready evidence:

- Complete approved Hire Line Dancers profile
- Teaching video or observed audition
- Two professional or client references when available
- Demonstrated beginner cueing and room management
- Signed managed-program service standards
- Confirmed service area and recurring availability
- Required insurance and screening for the specific client
- Equipment capability or disclosed equipment needs
- Agreement to shared reporting, backup, and cancellation procedures
- Specialty readiness for older adults, youth, large groups, campuses, corporate events, or venues where applicable

Every sold program needs:

- One primary instructor
- One oriented backup
- Shared program notes
- Client-specific requirements recorded before session one
- A replacement decision tree

Do not sell aggressively in a metro until there are at least three qualified primary instructors and two backup-capable instructors. Maintain roughly 20 percent uncommitted instructor capacity.

## Instructor pay and agency economics

Target cost structure:

| Cost or contribution | Target share of service revenue |
| --- | ---: |
| Instructor, assistant, and ordinary travel | 62 to 70 percent |
| Payment and service-recovery reserve | About 4 percent |
| Direct program operations | 3 to 5 percent |
| Contribution before central overhead | 25 to 32 percent |

Reprice or decline a program projected below 25 percent contribution unless it has a documented strategic reason and a time-limited exception.

Recommended instructor terms:

- Fixed pay for each delivered session
- Biweekly payment after session confirmation
- Same base pay for an approved substitute
- $50 emergency-replacement premium when appropriate
- $100 renewal bonus after 12 successfully delivered sessions
- Paid orientation for unusual client requirements
- Travel beyond the standard local allowance quoted separately

## Cash and payment policy

Institutional net-30 or net-60 payment terms can create a gap between client payment and instructor pay.

Pilot rules:

- Collect setup fees before work begins.
- Prefer the first month or a meaningful deposit before session one.
- Require an approved purchase order when a client cannot prepay.
- Pay instructors on the published schedule rather than waiting indefinitely for the institution.
- Maintain six to eight weeks of committed instructor and delivery costs before expanding aggressively.
- Track days sales outstanding and cash reserve relative to scheduled instructor pay.

## Cancellation and replacement policy

The following is a proposed commercial policy for review, not legal language.

### Client cancellation

- Seven or more days before a session: one no-fee reschedule per 12-session term, subject to availability.
- Between 72 hours and seven days: 50 percent session charge and 50 percent instructor pay.
- Under 72 hours or client no-show: full session charge and full instructor pay.
- Documented facility closure or emergency: one additional reschedule credit per term.
- Unused credits expire within 60 days or at the end of the agreement.

### Instructor cancellation

- Hire Line Dancers may place a qualified, client-compatible substitute without changing the scope.
- If no suitable replacement is available, the client may choose a reschedule, credit, or refund for that session.
- A client may request a new primary instructor after the first session or a documented fit problem.
- Repeated late cancellations remove an instructor from the managed-program pool. This does not automatically affect the separate directory listing unless another policy or quality issue exists.

## Delivery workflow

1. **Qualify the buyer.** Confirm audience, objective, cadence, space, budget, decision process, and desired start.
2. **Select the offer.** Fit the buyer into a standard package before discussing customization.
3. **Collect site requirements.** Document insurance, screening, accessibility, sound, participant ages, contract, and procurement needs.
4. **Price for contribution.** Include travel, assistants, unusual reporting, and payment terms.
5. **Execute agreement and payment assurance.** Do not schedule without a signed scope and appropriate payment or purchase order.
6. **Match primary and backup.** Confirm recurring availability and client fit.
7. **Orient the delivery team.** Review site rules, audience needs, session framework, incident process, and shared notes.
8. **Launch.** Provide approved client copy, first-session run of show, and named contacts.
9. **Track every session.** Record attendance, delivery status, notes, incidents, and substitutions.
10. **Report and renew.** Send a concise monthly recap and discuss renewal before the final two sessions.

## Staffing and capacity

Founding assumptions:

- The founder can manage approximately eight to ten active accounts while also selling.
- Add a part-time coordinator around eight accounts or 35 monthly sessions.
- Move to a full-time program manager around 15 accounts.
- One program manager can support approximately 20 to 25 active accounts or 80 to 100 monthly sessions after the workflow is standardized.
- Add part-time quality and recruiting support around 40 to 50 active instructors.

A program manager with 20 accounts should plan for roughly:

- 30 hours per month for account communication and reporting
- 14 hours for session monitoring
- 5 to 10 hours for replacements and exceptions
- 10 hours for billing and reconciliation
- 15 hours for onboarding new accounts

These are assumptions to measure during the pilot. Actual exception volume will determine capacity.

## Risk and vendor readiness

Before HLD acts as the institution's prime contractor, legal, insurance, and accounting professionals should review the operating model in each jurisdiction.

Required review areas:

- Independent-contractor classification and agency structure
- General, professional, auto, workers' compensation, umbrella, and abuse or molestation coverage where applicable
- Additional-insured and certificate requirements
- Participant waivers and assumption-of-risk language
- Background screening, fingerprinting, safeguarding, and mandated reporting
- Accessibility and accommodation process
- Emergency and incident response
- Public-performance music licensing allocation
- Sales tax and service-tax treatment
- Payment terms, refunds, chargebacks, and reserves
- Participant data and privacy
- Photo, video, and marketing consent

Published university examples often require outside vendors to provide $1 million per occurrence and $2 million aggregate general liability and name the university as an additional insured [S3, S4, S5]. Yale publishes a $2 million vendor requirement [S10]. Requirements vary, so HLD should collect them rather than claim one universal standard.

Programs involving minors should remain out of the first pilot. Some institutions require background checks, special abuse coverage, and other safeguards even when an outside vendor is present only briefly [S5, S9].

- At least 95 percent of scheduled sessions delivered
- At least 60 percent of pilots convert to a three-month or longer agreement
- At least 25 percent direct contribution after measured program-management time

- Client satisfaction of at least 4.5 out of 5
- Instructor satisfaction of at least 4.3 out of 5
- Average payment collection within 30 days
- No unresolved safety or conduct incident

## Hey Sammy consumer acquisition layer

Hire Line Dancers should not build a separate national venue database. It should partner with Hey Sammy to offer free ZIP-based access to line-dancing venues and activities.

The intended flow is:

1. HLD visitor enters a ZIP code.
2. A partner deep link opens a free Hey Sammy line-dancing experience.
3. The visitor sees approved nearby line-dancing results without onboarding, login, trial, or payment.
4. Hey Sammy may offer broader categories and personalization through its normal access model.
5. A no-results state sends demand back into HLD through "Bring line dancing to my town," instructor recruitment, or the teaching course.

The current Hey Sammy web app has a canonical Line dancing category and city/category filtering, but the whole preview is password protected and user-facing ZIP-distance search is not implemented. The partnership therefore needs a purpose-built public route, scoped access, approved-only queries, ZIP resolution, geographic ranking, attribution, and truthful empty states.

The detailed product brief is [Hey Sammy Free Line Dancing Access](./hey-sammy-line-dancing-free-access-brief.md).

## Program in a box for instructors

This should be a later product created from operating evidence, not a speculative library of templates.

Build it only after HLD has delivered enough managed programs to know which tools repeatedly matter. A useful first version should contain five modules:

1. **Choose the offer.** One clear class or residency format, price, minimum enrollment, and break-even calculation.
2. **Get the venue.** A short prospect list method, outreach scripts, discovery questions, and proposal template.
3. **Launch the first program.** A 30-day checklist, room and sound plan, registration copy, and promotional assets.
4. **Deliver reliably.** A session framework, attendance sheet, incident process, substitute plan, and participant follow-up.
5. **Earn the renewal.** A simple client recap, feedback request, and renewal conversation.

The product should feel like a guided launch path, not a document dump. A $299 founding version could include the kit, one group launch clinic, and one proposal review. Pricing should be tested only after the managed-program pilot reveals actual value.

## Key risks

- **Model confusion:** Keep Managed Programs separate from the no-commission directory.
- **Cash timing:** Maintain enough reserve to pay instructors before slow institutional invoices arrive.
- **Weak local density:** Do not sell recurring coverage without backups.
- **Custom-work creep:** Make every exception visible in the price and margin review.
- **Instructor quality variance:** Use observed evidence, shared frameworks, and session feedback.
- **Seasonality:** Balance campuses, parks, venues, workplaces, residential, and senior-living calendars.
- **Procurement friction:** Start vendor onboarding early and measure cycle length by segment.
- **Direct bypass:** Retain clients through reliability, billing, reporting, and coverage rather than contractual friction alone.
- **Safety and compliance:** Collect client-specific requirements and verify documents without making universal claims.
- **Operational ceiling:** Market density and standardization matter more than lead volume.
