# Hire Line Dancers outreach and growth playbook

Last updated: August 2, 2026

## Current status

Automated outreach, backlink outreach, and outbound campaigns are paused. This document is a future operating playbook, not authorization to send messages or activate outreach software. Current work should focus on real instructor profiles, reliable inquiry delivery, and learning from direct conversations.

## Purpose

Build both sides of the marketplace without requiring Hire Line Dancers to manage instructor contracts or event payments.

The growth system has four reinforcing paths:

1. Recruit credible instructors and give each one a useful public profile.
2. Reach buyers who repeatedly plan events and programs.
3. Build relationships with well-connected event professionals who can introduce line dancing to their clients.
4. Help ordinary employees, guests, parents, and community members suggest line dancing from the bottom up.

The core promise is simple: a beginner-friendly instructor can turn a room full of people into participants and create a shared experience attendees remember.

## Operating principles

- Keep the instructor as the main character in instructor content.
- Make the idea easy to forward to the person who controls an event budget.
- Keep instructor discovery public. Require contact information only when an organizer starts an inquiry.
- Let instructors set their own rates, availability, contracts, insurance, invoices, deposits, and payment methods.
- Do not promise guaranteed work to instructors or partners.
- Do not imply that DJs, planners, venues, or instructors are interchangeable. Each one contributes a different part of the event.
- Prefer personal, useful outreach over high-volume automation.
- Record the source, personalization, consent status, and outcome of every contact.

## DJ and mobile entertainment partnership play

### Why DJs are a strong channel

DJs are hired early, hear what clients want, know local planners and venues, and often work several event categories. A line dance instructor can help a DJ get more people participating. The DJ continues to manage music and production. The instructor manages step-by-step group instruction.

The offer is collaboration, not resale:

- Mutual introductions when a request fits
- A coordinated event run of show
- A shared sound and microphone checklist
- A song request worksheet
- Joint educational content about how the two roles work together
- Permissioned event recaps
- Trackable partner links

Do not require the DJ to bundle, contract, collect payment for, or guarantee the instructor.

### Weekly operating rhythm

1. Research 25 high-fit organizations in one launch market.
2. Verify each organization on its official site.
3. Record one real personalization fact and the source URL.
4. Verify the public professional email when available.
5. Let an agent draft the message, then require human approval before the first send.
6. Send one individualized introduction and at most two useful follow-ups around days 6 and 13.
7. Stop immediately on a reply, opt-out, or clear lack of relevance.
8. Handle LinkedIn and Instagram discovery and messages manually.
9. Give interested partners a useful asset and a trackable referral link.
10. Review replies, inquiries, and bookings by audience and city before adding volume.

## Instructor profile launch kit

Every approved instructor should receive:

- Their public profile URL
- A short tracked URL and QR code
- One profile announcement graphic
- One Instagram Story frame
- Three editable short-video scripts
- A simple filming and B-roll guide
- Three caption templates
- A copy-and-paste employee referral message
- A local call to action
- A national directory call to action for viewers outside the service area
- Clear organic and paid-use permission choices

The instructor posts from their own account and tags Hire Line Dancers. The marketplace can repost approved content. This keeps the message authentic and makes the instructor, not the platform, the focus.

## Instructor-led social formats

### Format 1: I want to teach your next event

Purpose: generate direct local interest.

Length: 20 to 30 seconds.

Script:

> Hey, [City]. I’m [Name], and I want to teach line dancing at your next [event type]. I make it beginner-friendly, welcoming, and easy for the whole room to participate. If you are planning an event, or know the person who is, take a look at my profile and ask about your date.

### Format 2: Send this to the person who plans your events

Purpose: create a bottom-up employee and guest referral loop.

Length: 15 to 25 seconds.

Script:

> If you work at a company in [City], send this to the person who plans your events. Ask them, “Could we hire a line dance instructor for our next party?” You do not need a partner or any dance experience. I’ll handle the teaching. You bring the coworkers. My profile is linked here.

### Format 3: Watch a room become participants

Purpose: demonstrate the experience.

Length: 15 to 30 seconds.

On-screen hook: What can 20 minutes of line dancing do for a party?

Voiceover:

> Most people arrive thinking they cannot dance. Then we learn a few steps together, try them with music, and suddenly the room is participating. That is why I love teaching line dancing at events.

End card: instructor name, service area, and profile URL.

## Filming and B-roll guide

Capture vertically in 9:16 at 1080p or higher when possible.

Useful footage:

- Direct-to-camera introduction with clean audio
- Wide shot of the full group learning
- Medium shot of the instructor demonstrating
- Close shot of feet during a simple sequence
- Participants following the same steps
- Genuine smiles and reactions
- The group completing a sequence together
- The instructor greeting or thanking the group
- A clean portrait or closing shot for the call to action

Editing rules:

- Put the hook in the first two seconds.
- Keep most clips between one and three seconds.
- Add accurate captions for sound-off viewing.
- Show clear instruction before advanced dancing.
- Avoid footage that makes the activity look intimidating or highly technical.
- Use music licensed for the account and platform.
- Do not show recognizable guests, company logos, venue branding, or minors without appropriate permission.

## Tracking and attribution

Give every instructor and connector a clean public URL plus a trackable link.

Example instructor link:

```text
https://hirelinedancers.com/instructors/[slug]?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=instructor_social&utm_campaign=profile_launch&utm_content=corporate_v1
```

Example partner link:

```text
https://hirelinedancers.com/cities/[city]?ref=[partner-code]
```

A short redirect such as `hirelinedancers.com/go/[code]` will be easier to say, print, and convert into a QR code later.

Track:

- Kit delivery and instructor participation
- Reach, short-video completion, sends, shares, and tags
- Instructor profile visits and city searches
- Contact starts and completed inquiries
- Qualified inquiry rate and instructor response time
- Partner-introduced leads
- Self-reported confirmed bookings
- Completed events and repeat inquiries
- Permissioned post-event content received

Because the platform will not handle contracts or payments, ask the instructor one simple follow-up question: “Did this inquiry become a booking?”

## Content permission levels

Use three explicit permission levels:

1. Instructor profile only
2. Instructor profile plus Hire Line Dancers organic social and email
3. Paid advertising, requiring separate permission

The instructor retains ownership. The permission record should say whether Hire Line Dancers may crop, caption, resize, edit, and repost the content with credit. It should also record approved channels, expiration date, review requirements, participant releases, client and venue permission, and whether audio may be replaced.

Paid advertising permission is never implied by organic reposting permission.

## Outreach and platform compliance

- Commercial B2B email must follow CAN-SPAM requirements, including truthful sender information, an honest subject, a valid postal address, and a working opt-out process.
- Honor opt-outs immediately and suppress them everywhere.
- Authenticate the sending domain with SPF, DKIM, and DMARC before starting campaigns.
- Do not use HubSpot marketing email for contacts who have not opted in. Use individual sales outreach and record consent separately.
- Keep LinkedIn and Instagram outreach manual. Do not scrape profiles or automate invitations, messages, comments, or engagement.
- Do not export Google Maps business data into the prospect database. Discover a possibility, then verify it on the organization’s official website.
- Do not buy lists or submit repetitive messages through contact forms.
- A sending limit is not permission to send at that volume.

## Minimum readiness before promotion

Do not ask instructors and partners to send traffic to a broken funnel. Before the first coordinated launch, confirm:

- A real instructor profile can be published and updated.
- The profile has a working inquiry action.
- The inquiry captures the event type, date, location, group size, contact information, and message.
- The instructor receives the inquiry promptly.
- Hire Line Dancers can record whether contact started and whether the instructor reports a booking.
- Privacy, terms, independent-provider language, and content permissions are visible.
- The instructor’s tracked profile link resolves correctly.

## First six-week pilot

### Week 1

- Configure the branded inbox and domain authentication.
- Set up HubSpot fields, pipeline stages, and do-not-contact handling.
- Choose one launch city with at least one real instructor.
- Research ten DJs and ten planners from official business sites.

### Week 2

- Send five DJ messages and five planner messages after individual review.
- Interview two instructors and one DJ about event logistics.
- Finalize the first instructor launch kit.

### Week 3

- Publish the instructor’s profile announcement and first local video.
- Give the instructor the employee-forwarding message.
- Follow up with the first partner cohort.

### Week 4

- Publish the teaching-proof video.
- Add a second small partner cohort.
- Review positive replies, objections, profile visits, and inquiry starts.

### Week 5

- Publish the employee referral video.
- Create one DJ and instructor planning resource.
- Record which audience produced the strongest conversations.

### Week 6

- Decide whether to repeat, revise, or stop each message.
- Expand only the strongest audience and city combination.
- Do not buy additional outreach software until the manual pilot shows a real bottleneck.

## Success measures

Measure by city, audience, instructor, partner, and content format:

- Researched contacts and verified contact rate
- First contacts, replies, positive replies, and meetings
- Active partners and referrals sent or received
- Instructor participation and content permission rate
- Shares, sends, profile visits, and city searches
- Contact starts and completed inquiries
- Qualified inquiries and instructor response time
- Self-reported bookings and completed events
- Post-event content and repeat referrals

The primary business measure is confirmed instructor bookings. The mission measure is completed events and the number of people who participate in line dancing because the connection was made.
